# Exemplars: Opinion Pieces

**Use when:** the post type is `opinion-piece`.

**Objective:** Turn the user's stance on an idea into a short LinkedIn post that gets the
reader to look at something familiar in a new way and apply it to themselves.

**How to use:** Study all exemplars collectively. Do not map a draft to any single
exemplar; instead, learn what to avoid from Corrections and Revisions, and what to model from each Approved Post.

**Shared patterns:** Apply these guidelines to every opinion piece.

- The user's take is the post. Lead with it, not with background.
- Frame the take as a principle or concept, not a procedure.
- Carry key words and metaphors through the post, and check that no two lines contradict each other's metaphor.
- Generalize the user's personal examples so the reader sees themselves in them.
- Close with an encouraging imperative and a question about how the reader applies the idea, rather than inviting pushback on the premise.
- No emojis or @mentions unless the user asks for them.
- Companion graphics follow the default visual workflow in SKILL.md.

## Exemplar 1 -- Who knows what you know

**Audience:** `public` (set by the user; the closing imperative and question were the user's call)

### Agent's First Draft

Important: Do not imitate this copy. It shows the starting point, which required revision.

```text
It's not who you know, it's who knows what you know.

The old saying is usually about networking. I'd take it one step further. A connection only matters if the person on the other end understands what you can do and where you can add value.

You can know someone with real influence over your work. If they've never seen your skills in action, that relationship changes very little.

Knowing people opens the door. Being known for something gets you through it.

Is that a different point, or just networking in new words? Where would you push back?

#Networking #CareerGrowth #Visibility
```

### Corrections and Revisions

Challenges to the user's claims and changes to the draft, in the order they happened.

1. Personal example (drafting decision, kept)
   - Before: "I know the CTO of my company. But if he's not aware ... simply knowing him doesn't make a difference." (user's notes)
   - After: "You can know someone with real influence over your work."
   - Why: A story about the user's own standing points the post back at him. Generalize the example so the reader sees their own situation in it.

2. Hook
   - Before: "It's not who you know, it's who knows what you know."
   - After: "It's not what you know. It's not who you know. It's who knows what you know."
   - Why: Quoting the full saying makes it recognizable at once, so the twist lands harder. The cadence justifies three short sentences over one.

3. Framing the original saying
   - Before: "The old saying is usually about networking. I'd take it one step further."
   - After: "Knowing the right people is sound career advice, but acquaintance alone isn't enough."
   - Why: "Old saying" made the adage sound outdated. Affirm the original advice, then show what completes it, without the words "improvement" or "extension."

4. Concept, not procedure
   - Before: "There's one more step that makes it work."
   - After: (folded into "...but acquaintance alone isn't enough.")
   - Why: "Step" reads like a procedure. The take is a principle. "There's a second half that makes it work" fixed the framing but was later replaced.

5. Bridge word
   - Before: "Networking with the right people is sound career advice." (user's alternative)
   - After: "Knowing the right people is sound career advice..."
   - Why: "Knowing" connects directly to the hook. "Networking" was more concrete but lost that bridge. The user made the call.

6. Metaphor consistency
   - Before: "...but acquaintance alone doesn't get your foot in the door." (user's proposal)
   - After: "...but acquaintance alone isn't enough."
   - Why: The closer says "Knowing people opens the door." Saying acquaintance doesn't get you in contradicts it. Keep the door image for the closer and drop it here. "Only gets you so far" was skipped because it doubles up "only" with the next sentence.

7. Scope
   - Before: "real influence over your work"
   - After: "real influence over your career"
   - Why: "Career" is broader and matches the "career advice" framing.

8. Awareness
   - Before: "If they've never seen your skills in action..."
   - After: "...if they don't know what you're capable of..."
   - Why: Awareness comes from direct experience, referrals, reputation, and more. Firsthand observation is too narrow.

9. Antecedent
   - Before: "...the opportunity changes very little." (user's proposal)
   - After: "...that connection does little for you."
   - Why: "The opportunity" had nothing earlier to refer to, and opportunities don't "change." "That connection" ties back to paragraph 1.

10. Call to action
    - Before: "Is that a different point, or just networking in new words? Where would you push back?"
    - After: "Don't wait to be discovered. In what ways are you making your capabilities known?"
    - Why: The user wanted a motivating imperative and a question about how the reader applies the idea, not a debate about the premise. Rejected verbs: "promoting" (self-serving), "exposing" (negative edge), and "showcasing" (LinkedIn cliché). "Making known" echoes "who knows" in the hook and "being known" in the closer.

11. Word choice
    - Before: "making your abilities known"
    - After: "making your capabilities known"
    - Why: "Capabilities" is broader and echoes "what you're capable of." "Value" was rejected: it can read as self-worth or pricing, it is more abstract than the rest of the post, and paragraph 1 already covers it with "add value."

### Approved Post

Model this copy stylistically. It reflects the user's voice and approved wording.

```text
It's not what you know. It's not who you know. It's who knows what you know.

Knowing the right people is sound career advice, but acquaintance alone isn't enough. A connection only matters if the person on the other end understands what you can do and where you can add value.

You can know someone with real influence over your career, but if they don't know what you're capable of, that connection does little for you.

Knowing people opens the door. Being known for something gets you through it.

Don't wait to be discovered. In what ways are you making your capabilities known?

#Networking #CareerGrowth #Visibility
```
